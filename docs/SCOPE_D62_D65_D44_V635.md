## 0 · Premise (verified at v6.34, not assumed)

### 0.1 · D-62: STAY UNDER IRMAA can land over the cliff it targets, for two reasons

The `irmaaCap` policy (L5504–5513) sets the year's conversion so that the year's income lands on the first IRMAA threshold of the premium
year two years out:

```
conv = min(thr − (base + taxable SS + dividends), headroom)        // 5 passes, for the Social Security fixed point
conv = max(0, Math.round(conv))                                     // L5680, every policy
```

**Cause 1: the sale.** The year's tax bill is then paid by selling from the brokerage (the funding block, L5783–5813).
- That sale realizes a gain, and the gain goes into the lookback MAGI: `magiHist[yr] = magi + g` (L5803).
- On an ACA bridge year, the ACA-premium sale's gain is added too (L5886).
- The IRMAA charged two years later reads that figure (L5732–5745).

So the conversion is solved against one MAGI and charged against a larger one.

**Cause 2: the rounding.** `Math.round` can round the solved conversion up by up to 50¢. Because the solve lands exactly on the threshold,
rounding up puts MAGI cents over it, and the cliff charges the full tier for any amount over. This happens with no sale at all: with tax
withheld from the conversion, every crossing is under $1.

**The ACA strategy** (`acaCliff`, L5514–5670) handles cause 1. It does three things:
- estimates the year's whole bill for a candidate conversion;
- grosses the sale up as the funding block does, and subtracts the sale's gain from the headroom (`_estSaleGain`, L5565–5665, 8 passes);
- keeps a $500 safety margin (`ACA_CONSTS.solverMargin`, L2264), which also absorbs the rounding.

`irmaaCap` has none of this.

**It happens at a declared gain of 0%, the shipped default.** Engine A carries today's brokerage balance to the retirement year at
inflation but not its basis (v6.30, D-60: L5287, L5374), so any plan retiring after its as-of year has gain to realize.

**Measured** (instrumented copy, v6.34 module). "Over" means the lookback MAGI is above the first-tier threshold, by any amount, in a year
the strategy converts; "under $1" counts the rounding crossings among them.

| Household | Conversion years | Years over (under $1) | Largest |
|---|---|---|---|
| The example, shipped defaults (0%, paid from the brokerage) | 10 | 6 (4) | $2,916 |
| The example at 30% / 60% declared gain | 10 / 10 | 6 (5) / 6 (5) | $13,841 / $24,641 |
| The example, tax withheld from the conversion (30%) | 10 | 5 (5) | under $1 |
| `t3`'s household (married, Georgia, retiring 2027), 0% / 50% / 80% | 7 / 6 / 6 | 5 / 5 / 5 | $8,224 / $28,717 / $43,352 |
| `t3`'s household, withheld (50%) | 6 | 2 (2) | under $1 |
| `t3`'s household made single, 50% | 11 | 11 | $12,966 to $19,219 |
| All 17 fixture households, 0% and 40% | 2 to 11 each | 1 to 8 each; every one crosses | $6,744 at 0%; $17,442 at 40% |

On `t3`'s household, STAY UNDER IRMAA pays **$10,645** of lifetime IRMAA (**$9,200** today) where NO CONVERSIONS pays **$0**. With the tax
withheld, the two sub-dollar crossings alone cost **$3,986**.

On the example, STAY UNDER IRMAA pays $13,800 today against $8,050 for NO CONVERSIONS. With the tax withheld it pays $11,500, all of the
difference from rounding.

**Direction: mis-solved, not optimistic.** Engine A charges the surcharge the crossing causes (L5744). So the row's tax, IRMAA, estate and
break-even figures are consistent with what the row does. What is wrong is the strategy:
- it converts slightly more than a strategy named *stay under* should;
- it pays a surcharge its name says it avoids.

A correct solver converts a little less in those years and pays no surcharge for them; the net change on the row varies by plan. It
reaches the Roth tab's strategy table and the optimizer grid's *UNDER IRMAA* cell (L7320), and nothing else: no other tab reads
`irmaaCap`.

**Disclosed since v6.29** in one sentence on the Roth tab (L11375): *"…converts up to the first surcharge cliff on income before the
year's tax-paying sale — a gain that sale realizes can still lift MAGI over it."*
- No suite asserts that sentence (`suitecensus.cjs`).
- The Field Manual and METHODOLOGY do not mention it.
- No surface mentions the rounding.

### 0.2 · D-65: the first two premium years after retirement are charged nothing

Medicare sets a year's premium from the MAGI of two years before. The first two premium years after retirement — the retirement year and
the year after — are therefore set by the last two working years.

**No engine charges them.** By census, IRMAA is charged at exactly three sites (`irmaaSurchargeFor`): Engine A's estimate L5562, Engine A's
charge L5744, and Engine C's L6144.
- **Engine A** (the Roth tab, What Breaks):
  - its lookback `magiHist` is created empty (L5380) and filled from the retirement year (the loop at L5396);
  - for the retirement year and the next, `magiHist[yr − 2]` is undefined, so `irmaa` stays 0.
- **Engine C** (the IRMAA tab; the Monte Carlo through `mcIncomeTaxSchedule`, L122–135):
  - its rows start at the retirement year (L6045), and each row bills its premium year two years later (L6131);
  - its first premium year is therefore retirement + 2;
  - `t94` A-0 and A-4 pin exactly this, and the Income tab's card (L8994) is built on it.

**Who it affects.** A household with someone on Medicare in the retirement year or the year after, whose working income was above the
first tier: about $218,000 joint or $109,000 single, at 2026 thresholds.
- The example is not one. It holds no working MAGI; its prompt describes about $162,000 of gross pay, under the tier either way.
- Its Medicare dates (A in January 2029, the retirement year; B in 2031) mean one person would be billed in both years.

**Measured** (v6.34 module; the published tables, the plan's inflation, the example's calendar, one person billed in each year):

| Last working years' MAGI (today's $) | Tier | Uncharged, two years together (today's $) |
|---|---|---|
| $180,000 | standard | $0 |
| $250,000 | 1 | $2,300 |
| $300,000 | 2 | $5,760 |
| $400,000 | 3 | $9,240 |
| $600,000 | 4 | $12,720 |

A couple both on Medicare in those years would see twice these.

**The form that can remove it.** Form SSA-44 lets a new retiree ask Social Security to use current-year income instead. Work stoppage is
one of the qualifying events; a Roth conversion is not. The model cannot know whether a household will file it, so the question is which
way to err (DW-2).

**Disclosed since v6.31** in:
- the IRMAA tab (L11953), the Monte Carlo tab (L12062) and What Breaks (L12786);
- four Field Manual sentences (L5169), one of them the v6.31 release-history entry;
- METHODOLOGY: *The Monte Carlo pays IRMAA (v6.31)*, and §8's note at L1316.

`t94` G-2, G-3, G-10 and G-12 assert that copy. `t31` D-1 to D-3 assert that the IRMAA tab and the Field Manual name SSA-44.

### 0.3 · D-44: a one-time row dated before the retirement year never reaches three engines

By census, `tableYearExpense` has callers only at L6300, L12411 and L12646. Three engines spend the expense table year by year from the
retirement date, starting from **today's holdings**:
- **Engine D** (the Withdrawal tab):
  - its Priority-1 pool is `PORTFOLIO.household − PORTFOLIO.total401k` (L6260);
  - its buckets are today's bucket shares of `total401k` (L6277–6280);
  - both are carried to the retirement year at inflation (L6345–6346);
  - its spending starts at the retirement year (`annualExpense`, L6300).
- **Backtest**: `startBal = PORTFOLIO.household` (L12397), spending from the retirement year (L12411).
- **What Breaks**: `startBal = PORTFOLIO.household` (L12628), spending from the retirement year (L12646).

None of the three subtracts a one-time row dated between today and the retirement year. The two Monte Carlo engines do: they run from the
as-of month and charge every one-time row in its quarter, before and after retirement, grown with the run's inflation. L3068 and L3298
sit outside `if (retired)`; this was closed at v6.20.

**What the Priority-1 pool holds.** `total401k` is the sum of the positions (L4671–4675), so the pool holds the Other accounts only.
- On the example that is $147,000:
  - $111,000 Traditional IRA, plan and annuity money;
  - $15,000 HSA;
  - **$21,000 of after-tax brokerage** — the only part that is the household's after-tax savings.
- The positions, and so the buckets, are entirely Traditional and Roth, about 79% and 21%.

**Visible on the example, at the +1 YEAR button.** The example has no such row at its planned retirement (2029). Its six one-time rows are:
- two in April 2029, the retirement year, which Engine D charges in-year;
- four replacement cars from 2037.

At the **+1 YEAR** button (2030, offered by `buildRetireOptions`), the two April 2029 rows ($20,000 golf cart, $8,000 closing costs) fall
before retirement:
- the Withdrawal tab's schedule is identical with and without them (`probe_d44c.mjs`: end balance $2,098,139 either way);
- Backtest and What Breaks never read them;
- the Monte Carlo at 2030 does charge them.

**Confirmed on another row:** a $30,000 row added to the example in May 2027 changes neither Engine D's schedule nor the Monte Carlo's tax
schedule (`probe_d44.mjs`, `probe_d44b.mjs`).

**An approximation of the fix.** Today's holdings, reduced by the $30,000 — close to, but not, the recommended rule:
- Engine D's after-tax pool at the end of 2029, after that year's draw and growth, is $33,656 lower;
- its 2053 end balance is $84,704 lower;
- the Monte Carlo's tax schedule rises by $1,336 over the plan, because Engine D's draws reach Engines A, B and C (§0.4 item 4).

**Direction: optimistic,** by the row's amount and the growth it would have lost.

**Disclosed since v6.18** on the Withdrawal tab (L10562), in the Field Manual's Withdrawal Strategy entry (L5169), and in METHODOLOGY (*The
Withdrawal tab spends the expense table year by year (v6.18)*, L2634, and the v6.20 section's L2826).
- `t81` E-1 asserts the tab's sentence.
- Backtest and What Breaks say nothing about it.

### 0.4 · What the register and my summary got wrong

1. **D-62 is not optimistic** (§0.1, *Direction*). My summary for Steve on 2026-10-28 listed it as one of three items where the model
   flatters a plan, and so did the spreadsheet of open items (its Summary sheet). The register row gives no direction; it says only "Low".
   At the v6.35 build the row gains: *mis-solved, two causes (the sale's gain, and rounding); its cost is charged*.
2. **D-62's candidate fix does not work as written.** *"Solve with the sale's gain included (as the ACA strategy does)"* oscillates on ACA
   bridge years whenever the IRMAA target sits near the ACA cliff — a single filer in a household of three or four (§6, B1). The design in
   §1.1 replaces the fixed-point loop with a search.
3. **D-44's fix site.** The register, and the v6.20 scope's DC-5, say fixing it means *"taking the row out of `retireStartBalances`, which
   Engines A, B, C and D all share"*. `retireStartBalances` (L2830–2902) builds only the Traditional and Roth balances and their shares; it
   holds no taxable money and reads no expense. Engine D's after-tax pool, and the Backtest and What Breaks balances, come from
   `PORTFOLIO.household` directly (§0.3).
4. **D-44's reach.**
   - The register names only the Withdrawal tab. Backtest and What Breaks start from the same holdings and have never said so.
   - Engine D's draws reach Engines B and C through the gains bridge (`withdrawalPlanSeries`), and Engine A through `ordDrawByYr`, which
     all four Engine A callers pass (L7313, L11331, L11479, L12692; `census.cjs`). So the fix also moves the Taxes, IRMAA and Roth tabs and
     What Breaks' tax estimate for a plan with such a row. That is correct and intended.

## 1 · What changes (the recommended shape)

### 1.1 · D-62 (DI-1 (a), DI-2 (a))

- **One estimate of the year's MAGI for a candidate conversion, shared by both cliff strategies.** The pieces move out of the `acaCliff`
  branch to year scope, unchanged in body:
  - the current-year IRMAA term (L5552–5564);
  - `_estSaleGain` (L5565–5665);
  - the effective gain fraction (L5543);
  - the guard (L5549) and the ages (L5551).

  It takes one more argument: the MAGI cap at which the ACA subsidy is evaluated. `cliff` enters only through that cap (L5648), so:
  - `acaCliff` passes `cliff − ACA_CONSTS.solverMargin` and keeps its own 8-pass loop exactly as now. Its results must stay
    **bit-identical** (§3 A-2).
  - The ACA term gains an explicit `acaHeads(yr, widowed) > 0` gate. It is harmless today (the baseline subsidy is undefined off the
    bridge), but it makes the dependence visible.
- **`irmaaCap` searches instead of iterating.** The estimate, *MAGI(c)*, never decreases as the conversion *c* grows: taxable Social
  Security, the bill, the sale and the lost ACA subsidy all rise with it. So `irmaaCap` takes the **largest whole-dollar conversion whose
  MAGI(c) is at or below the threshold less the margin**, found by bisection on [0, headroom].
  - It is rounded down, never up, so cause 2 cannot recur.
  - A jump in MAGI(c) at the ACA cliff cannot make it oscillate: a search has no phases.
  - The gain includes the ACA-premium sale's on a bridge year, because the lookback includes it (L5886).
  - Withholding is handled as the estimate already handles it: only the part of the bill the conversion does not absorb reaches the
    brokerage. With withholding, the sale's gain falls as the conversion grows, but MAGI(c) still rises — and §3 A-1 tests it.
  - **If the build finds MAGI(c) decreasing anywhere, it stops and reports** (scope rule) rather than adapting.
- **A $500 margin** (DI-2 (a)), as a named constant of its own beside `ACA_CONSTS`, not a reuse of the ACA one. The two cliffs are
  unrelated, so a change to one should not move the other.
- **Engine A returns `magiByYr`**, a copy of its lookback MAGI, so a test can see the figure the surcharge reads. Additive; no caller
  uses it.
- **Copy.**
  - The Roth tab sentence becomes: *"STAY UNDER IRMAA converts the most it can while keeping the year's MAGI — the gain from the sale
    that pays the year's tax included — at least $500 under the first surcharge cliff."*
  - The Field Manual's Roth section gains the same sentence.
  - METHODOLOGY §7 gains a short section: the rule, both causes, the measurements in §0.1, and that the row's cost was always charged.

### 1.3 · D-44 (DE-1 (a), DE-2 (a), DE-3 (a), DE-4 (a))

- **One helper, `preRetireOneTime(retireYr)`.** It returns, in today's dollars, the sum of the one-time rows dated from the as-of month
  through December of the year before `retireYr` (DE-2 (a)), read through the existing `getOneTimeExpenses`.
  - Rows before the as-of month are history: today's holdings already reflect them.
  - It is the tab's selected retirement year, so the +1 YEAR button gets its own answer.
- **Engine D** subtracts the helper's total from today's money **before** the carry (L6345), so the reduction reaches the retirement year
  at inflation like everything else (DE-4 (a)):

     rata, so the gain share is unchanged.
  2. The rest from the buckets, pro rata across b1 to b4. That takes Traditional and Roth money in the buckets' own shares, with **no tax
     charged**: the model does not project working years. This is disclosed in-app and in METHODOLOGY. It is the same simplification the
     Monte Carlo makes, which spends the row from one total.
  - The order is fixed:
    - `tradFrac` and `rothFrac` (L6340–6341) are taken from the buckets before the subtraction. Pro rata leaves their ratio unchanged,
      and the trad and Roth notionals shrink with the buckets.
    - `taxOrd = min(_taxOrdInit, _taxInit)` (L6317) is computed on the unreduced pool. The subtraction never reaches the Traditional,
      annuity, HSA or Roth parts of the pool.
  - Never more than the money there: a row larger than everything clamps at 0.
- **Backtest and What Breaks** start from `max(0, PORTFOLIO.household − preRetireOneTime(retireYear))`. Both run in today's prices from
  the retirement date, so no carry is needed. Backtest's *"against your $X.XXM"* sentence (L12549) names the reduced start, and, when the
  helper is non-zero, says why.
- **Engines A, B and C's own starting pools are not changed** (DE-3 (a)). They do not spend the expense table; their after-tax pool
  (`taxableInitAll`) sets dividend income and pays conversion tax, and stays at today's holdings. METHODOLOGY says so. Their figures still
  move for such plans, through Engine D's draws (§0.4 item 4).
- **Copy.**
  - The Withdrawal tab's sentence (L10562) becomes: *"…a one-time row dated before the retirement year is paid from your savings before
    it: the schedule starts from today's holdings less those rows, after-tax money first, with no tax on anything taken from your

  - Backtest and What Breaks get the same clause, shorter.
  - So do the Field Manual's Withdrawal Strategy entry and METHODOLOGY's v6.18 section. METHODOLOGY's v6.20 *"stays open"* line is closed.

## 5 · Decisions for Steve

**DI-1 · D-62: fix the solver?**
- **(a) Recommended: yes — share the ACA strategy's estimate, and search instead of iterating** (§1.1). One piece of tax arithmetic
  instead of two copies that drift. The ACA strategy comes out bit-identical, and a test holds it. The search is what makes it safe on
  bridge years, where copying the ACA loop as it stands fails.
- (b) Leave it disclosed, and add the rounding to the disclosure. Nothing is overstated, because the row's cost is charged, but the
  strategy's name stays untrue on the example at the defaults.

**DI-2 · D-62: a safety margin**
- **(a) Recommended: $500 below the threshold**, as the ACA strategy uses. A cent over costs a full tier, and the estimate agrees with the
  engine to within cents, not exactly. Every plan's STAY UNDER IRMAA row converts up to $500 a year less, far smaller than the crossings in
  §0.1.
- (b) No margin: the search to the threshold exactly, rounded down. This now works on paper, because the search never rounds up. But it
  leaves no room for the estimate to differ from the engine by a cent, and a cent over costs a full tier.

**DW-1 · D-65: where the working years' income comes from**
- **(a) Recommended: one optional My Data figure, in today's dollars, used for both lookback years** (§1.2). It is on the household's tax
  return (or will be), it is one line to fill in, and it is the input the register proposed.
- (b) Two figures, one per year. More exact for someone whose last year differs (a part year, a bonus), but a second field few will know
  how to fill differently.
- (c) Derive it from the salaries My Data holds, less pre-tax contributions, plus dividends. No new field, but it is an estimate of an
  estimate. It charges a surcharge from a number the user never saw, and does nothing for the example, which holds no salary.
- (d) Leave it disclosed.

**DW-2 · D-65: when the figure is entered, charge it in full?**
- **(a) Recommended: yes, in full, with the SSA-44 sentence kept beside it.** That is the conservative direction, and it is what happens if
  the household does not file the form.
- (b) Add an *"I will file SSA-44"* switch that zeroes the two years. Rejected in §4.

**DW-3 · D-65: how the IRMAA tab shows them**
- **(a) Recommended: two rows at the top of the year-by-year table, labelled "· your entry"**, so the lifetime figure visibly adds up.
- (b) One line above the table. Smaller, but the total would include figures the table does not show.

**DW-4 · D-65: a lookback year that is already past**

For a plan retiring this year or next, one or both lookback years are at or before the as-of year: their returns are filed, or nearly.
- **(a) Recommended: take the figure as entered for those years, and carry it forward only for later years.** A past return is in its own
  year's dollars; deflating it would charge a lower tier than the return shows.
- (b) Carry both ways, from today's dollars. One rule, but it understates a past year.

**DW-5 · D-65: the blank field is the optimistic default**
- (a) Blank charges nothing, with the pointer to the field (as recommended in an earlier draft).
- **(b) Recommended: the same, plus a one-line flag when the salaries on My Data add up to more than the first-tier threshold** (§1.2).
  No figure changes, but the case where blank is most likely wrong is no longer silent. It is the project's rule: simplifications are
  disclosed, and the conservative direction is the default.
- (c) Charge an estimate from the salaries when the field is blank. Rejected for DW-1 (c)'s reasons.

**DE-1 · D-44: in this release or not**
- **(a) Recommended: in.** It is one helper and three starting balances, not the four-engine change the register described. It moves the
  example only at the +1 YEAR button, where today $28,000 of spending is simply lost. It closes D-44, including the Backtest and What
  Breaks gaps found here.
- (b) Out, to its own release, with Backtest and What Breaks added to the disclosure.

**DE-2 · D-44: which rows**
- **(a) Recommended: one-time rows from the as-of month through December of the year before retirement.** That is where the Monte Carlo's
  first quarter counts from. Rows already past are in today's holdings.
- (b) Every one-time row before the retirement year, past ones included. That double-counts anything already spent.

**DE-3 · D-44: which engines**
- **(a) Recommended: the three that spend the table — the Withdrawal tab, Backtest, What Breaks.** The tax engines still move through the
  bridge (§0.4 item 4).
- (b) Also reduce Engines A, B and C's own after-tax pool. Fuller, but it moves every Roth strategy and the Taxes and IRMAA tabs'
  dividends for a second-order effect.

**DE-4 · D-44: which money pays it, in the Withdrawal tab**

On the example, the after-tax money is $21,000 of a $147,000 first pool (§0.3), so this matters.
- **(a) Recommended: after-tax brokerage first, then the buckets pro rata, untaxed, and disclosed.** It is what a household would spend
  first. The rest comes out of retirement money in its own Traditional/Roth shares, with the tax that would really be owed not charged —
  the same simplification the Monte Carlo makes. Taking after-tax money first is the more conservative order: it leaves more Traditional
  money to be taxed later.
- (b) After-tax first, then the Traditional IRA money in the first pool (`taxOrd`), then the buckets. That spends IRA money ahead of plan
  money for no reason the model can state.
- (c) After-tax money only; anything beyond it is not charged, disclosed. Simpler, but optimistic by exactly the overflow — $9,000 of the
  $30,000 on the example.

**DV-1 · When the tax figures go first**
- **(a) Recommended: the day the IRS TY2027 revenue procedure, the §402(g) notice and CMS's 2027 IRMAA figures are all published, if v6.35
  is not yet in its build review.** These are the three sources the refresh's DT-7 and `package_check` name. The 2027 work then takes v6.35,
  and this release becomes v6.36, renumbered in that package (§1.4). If v6.35 is already in its build review, it finishes first.
- (b) A fixed date, 1 December, whatever is published.
