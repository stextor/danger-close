**In one paragraph.** When spouse B retires after spouse A, B keeps contributing in the years between. Through v6.43 the app counts
those contributions three different ways:
- the **tax engines** (the Taxes, IRMAA and Roth tabs, and the Monte Carlo's tax through them) put all of them in the balance at A's
  retirement, so they grow for one to several years before they are made;
- the **Withdrawal tab, What Breaks and Backtest** leave them out entirely (v6.43's DD-7);
- the **Monte Carlo** adds them quarter by quarter until B retires.

The recommended fix adds a later spouse's contributions in the year they are made, in every deterministic projection. Measured on 20
plans for every projection except What Breaks and Backtest:
- the Withdrawal tab's end balance rises by $8,800 to $178,000 in today's dollars;
- lifetime tax moves by −$6,004 to +$2,213 — mostly down, because the tax engines stop counting returns on money not yet contributed;
- the Monte Carlo's success rate moves by −0.3 to +0.1 points;
- the Roth tab's estate-maximising cell does not change on any of the 20 plans.

D-72 and D-73 each need one guard, for a single plan restored from a backup that still carries spouse-B data.

### 0.1 · D-71: three readings of the same contributions

`contribAccrual(retireYr)` (L2881) counts each spouse's planned contributions from the as-of year to that spouse's retirement year:
- A runs to the selected year;
- B runs to `targetRetireYearB`.

Its one engine-side caller, `retireStartBalances` (L3028), puts the sum into the balances at **A's** retirement year. Engines A, B and C
start from those balances, carried by `carryStartBalances` with the accrual at face value:
- Engine A, `runRothStrategies`, L5541 — through three P builders: L7663, L11737, L11887;
- Engine C, `computeIrmaaPlan`, L6284;
- Engine B, `computeTaxPlan`, L7234.

The Roth ladder (L11180) and its RMD cards (L11383) start the same way. So a B who retires in 2035 has 2029–2034's contributions in the
2029 balance, where each earns one to six years of returns before it is made, and all of them count toward the RMD base.

v6.43's `openingAccrual` (L2914) passes `bStopsAtA`, so Engine D, What Breaks and Backtest count B only to A's year. The rest are left
out, disclosed through `laterBLeftOut` (L2935). It has three readers:
- `retireStartNote` (*"B's only to 2029"*, L2943);
- the Withdrawal tab's method note (L10972);
- the Monte Carlo tab's method note (L12482).

The Field Manual's Withdrawal entry and its v6.43 §13 note also say so, inside `DOCS_HTML` (L5367).

The Monte Carlo adds B's monthly contribution (`spouseBMonthly`, today's dollars × `contribRate`) each quarter before `spouseRetireQ`,
which comes from `targetRetireYearB` (`runExtendedMC` L3333/L3338/L3434, `runMonteCarlo` L3157/L3158/L3233).

**The example cannot show D-71.** Its spouse B retires with A (both 2029), so every figure below is $0 on it, which is why the census
gains a row (§3.3). The example does carry built-in spouse-B wages in A's first three retirement years (`spouseBWorkTaper`, v6.23, §4).

### 0.5 · The measurement prototype, and what it does not cover

`s644/proto.py` builds `v643x.jsx` with a mode switch:
- **`cap`** passes `bStopsAtA` to `retireStartBalances`' accrual.
- **`inyear`** adds, on top of `cap`, B's contributions at face value at the end of each year from A's retirement to the year before
  B's (never before the as-of year), in four engines:
  - Engines B and C: pre-tax to B's Traditional, the annuity and employer shares re-scaled;
  - Engine A: pre-tax to B's Traditional and Roth to B's Roth;
  - Engine D: both to the buckets by the contribution mix, with the shares and B's part of the Traditional re-taken.
  - Nothing is added after a rollover, or after B's death.
- **`Duncap`** lets `openingAccrual` run B to B's own year: an upper bound for Engine D, kept for comparison.

**Null control.** With the switch unset, the prototype's output is identical on all 22 plans, on every field the probe reads, to the
v6.43 module's. The review re-ran it independently. The review also instrumented the step: it adds in 2029–2034 on the +6-year plan
and in 2026–2031 on the retired plan; in 2026–2027 on a plan whose retirement year (2024) is before its as-of year; never twice.

**Not prototyped:**
- **What Breaks and Backtest.** Their loops carry the balance in start-relative nominal units, so the step there must be designed
  (§1.2), not copied.
- **The Roth ladder and its RMD cards.**
- **The prototype is not the fix.** It reads `PORTFOLIO` inside Engine A, which §1.1 forbids.

## 1 · What changes (the recommended shape: DE-1 (a) to DE-6 (a))

### 1.1 · One helper for a later spouse's contributions (D-71)

`laterBContribAt(yr, retireYr)` returns `{ trad, roth }`: spouse B's planned contributions in year `yr`, at face value (today's
monthly amounts × 12, the v5.10 rule — no growth, no raises).

It returns them only when all of these hold:
- the plan is married;
- `yr` is on or after `retireYr` — the engine's own retirement year, the one the ±1 selector moves, not `tl.targetRetireYear`;
- `yr` is before `targetRetireYearB` and on or after the as-of year;
- B is alive in `yr`.

A negative contribution adds nothing, as at v6.43.

`retireStartBalances` counts B's accrual only to A's year. It passes `bStopsAtA`, as `openingAccrual` already does, so the two
readings become one. `contribAccrual`'s default (B to B's own year) stays for the My Data readout, which states what B will have
contributed by then (`t7`'s $72,000 is unaffected).

**Engine A reads its inputs from P, never from module state.** L5560–5568 records why: a global read inside it would make hand-built
households depend on state they never set. So the three P builders (L7663, L11737, L11887) pass the series, `laterB: { [yr]: { trad,
roth } }`. A P without it adds nothing. Engine A's pooled path (`P.tradInitA === undefined`, L5539) has no spouse-B Traditional and
adds nothing.

### 1.2 · Every deterministic projection adds them in the year made (DE-1 (a))

At the end of year `yr`, after that year's growth, each projection adds `laterBContribAt(yr)`. The money grows from the next year on
(DE-2 (a)).

- **Engines B and C:** to B's Traditional (pre-tax only — neither carries a Roth balance), the annuity share re-scaled dollar for
  dollar. The employer share is re-taken over B's non-annuity Traditional: B's contributions are IRA money by v5.93's conservative
  default. The prototype's simpler rescale is exact only when B holds no annuity.
- **Engine A:** to B's Traditional and Roth, from P.
- **Engine D:** to the buckets by the contribution mix, as v6.43 places the opening contributions. The Traditional and Roth shares and
  B's part of the Traditional are re-taken over the new total.
- **The Roth ladder (L11180):** the same step in its year loop.
- **Its RMD cards (L11383):** closed-form. `noConvTrad = t0·(1+g)^yrs` (L11464–11465) and the extrapolation in `balAt` / `annAt`
  (L11446–11454) need the same contributions, or `rmdReduction` (L11473) compares a path with them against one without. Each card's
  formula gets B's contributions grown from their year.
- **What Breaks and Backtest:** their loops carry `bal` in start-relative nominal units — today's dollars at the start, grown by
  `cumInf` (What Breaks `project`, L13077–13086; Backtest's path, L12856–12871, with historical inflation). A face-value contribution
  made in year `yr` is `c ÷ planDollarFactor(yr, asOf)` today's dollars, so it enters the loop as that × the loop's `cumInf`. In
  Backtest that holds for every historical start.
- **After the first death:** nothing is added once B has died. If A has died, B's contributions continue to B's retirement.

**The Monte Carlo is unchanged** except for D-73. It already adds B's contributions in these years, in today's dollars × `contribRate`,
quarterly. So after the fix every projection agrees on **which years** carry B's contributions. The deterministic engines add face value
(nominal) and the Monte Carlo today's dollars, so the amounts differ by inflation, as v6.30's DU-7 (a) already accepts for the accrual.

### 1.3 · D-72: on a single plan a B-owned stream is the one person's (DE-4 (a))

In `streamsMonthlyAt`, a stream whose stored owner is B is read as A's on a single plan, for both the alive test and the `opts.owner`
filter. A query for owner B then returns 0, and one for owner A includes it, so `workEarners`' one-person sum counts it once.

The three labels (L9216, L9322, L14413) name the one person on a single plan.

The stored owner is not changed, as D-70 decided for holdings: a plan switched back to married keeps its split.

### 1.4 · D-73: the Monte Carlo ignores spouse B's contribution on a single plan (DE-5 (a))

`runMonteCarlo` and `runExtendedMC` take `spouseBMonthly` as 0 when the timeline is single, as `getSSB()` already does for B's benefit.
The stored field is untouched.

## 5 · Decisions for Steve

**DE-1 · Which projections add a later spouse's contributions in the year made?**
- **(a) All of them (recommended):** Engines A to D, the Roth ladder and its RMD cards, What Breaks and Backtest.
  - Every projection then counts the same contributions in the same years as the Monte Carlo.
  - The Withdrawal tab's end balance rises on such plans (+$8,800 to +$178,000 in today's dollars on the 20 measured). Lifetime tax
    moves −$6,004 to +$2,213 and Monte Carlo success −0.3 to +0.1 points. No estate cell changes; two lowest-tax cells do.
  - It replaces v6.43's DD-7. DD-7's reason was that counting the contributions from the start would let them earn returns before they
    are made; the in-year rule meets that exactly.
- **(b) The tax engines only (A, B, C, the ladder and its cards):** Engine D, What Breaks and Backtest keep DD-7 (left out, disclosed).
  Smaller release. The tax engines' error goes (−$74 to −$5,271), and the Withdrawal tab's larger, conservative gap stays.
- **(c) Cap the tax engines at A's year, matching Engine D:** cheapest, one argument. It drops money the plan will hold: tax $1,168 to
  $23,239 low, 16 best-cell changes, Monte Carlo success up to 1.75 points high. Not recommended.
- **(d) Disclose only:** D-71 closes as a disclosed simplification; D-72 and D-73 could ride along with the 2027 tax release.

**DE-2 · When in the year does a contribution enter?**
- **(a) At the end of the year it is made (recommended),** at face value, growing from the next year. This is the v5.10 face-value rule,
  and the conservative choice.
- **(b) At the start of the year:** one more year of growth per contribution.

**DE-3 · Spouse B's wages between the two retirements (§4).**
- **(a) File a new MissingFeatures item and disclose (recommended).**
- (b) Deduct B's pre-tax contribution from B's job rows in those years. A job row may be a different job, so this guesses.

**DE-4 · D-72, a single plan's B-owned stream.**
- **(a) Read it as the one person's in `streamsMonthlyAt` and the three labels, stored owner untouched (recommended),** as D-70 did for
  holdings.
- (b) Rewrite the owner at load.

**DE-5 · D-73, spouse B's contribution on a single plan.**
- **(a) The Monte Carlo reads it as 0 on a single plan, stored field untouched (recommended),** as `getSSB()` does.
- (b) Zero it at load.
